# 9. Height zero characters of a block

## Status
ROADMAP-PR. Open PR #2411 (ModularCharactersAndBlocksPartII) specifies the definition: §6.3 defines the height `h(χ) = ν(χ(1)) − (a − d)`, the defect `d(χ)` and `Irr₀(B)`, with independence of the defect group, `Irr₀(B) ≠ ∅` and the change of place and level; §6.2 proves Brauer's Theorem 9.1 (`brauer_height`: `p^{a−d} ∣ χ(1)`, minimum attained), on central characters of blocks, Osima's formula and defect classes (§6.1); §9.1 and §9.2 state Brauer's height zero and the Alperin–McKay conjectures as propositions. It consumes `Irr(B)`, defect groups and Brauer correspondents from merged [RepresentationTheory/ModularCharactersAndBlocks](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/RepresentationTheory/ModularCharactersAndBlocks/README.md) (§§4.2, 6.2, 7.1), which does not define heights. No merged roadmap specifies the definition. Depends on #2 and #3.

## Prerequisite chain
1. p-adic valuation of integers, character degrees: Mathlib / Tau Ceti.
2. `Irr(G,b)`: ModularCharactersAndBlocks §4.2 (merged; see #2).
3. Defect group `D` and `|D|` well defined up to conjugacy: ModularCharactersAndBlocks §6.2 (merged; see #3).
4. Central characters of blocks, Osima's formula, defect classes: PR #2411 §6.1.
5. Brauer's Theorem 9.1 (`|G:D|_p ∣ χ(1)`, minimum attained): PR #2411 §6.2.
6. Height `h(χ)` and `Irr₀(G,b)`: PR #2411 §6.3.
7. Brauer correspondent for the local side of Alperin–McKay: ModularCharactersAndBlocks §7.1 (merged); the Alperin–McKay and height zero statements: PR #2411 §§9.1–9.2.

## Library-scale gap
Covered by merged ModularCharactersAndBlocks (the inputs) and PR #2411 (heights) together (Navarro, *Characters and Blocks of Finite Groups*, Ch. 3–4; Feit, *The Representation Theory of Finite Groups*, Ch. IV): what remains for the definition is review and merging of PR #2411. PR #2411 states the height zero conjecture as a proposition with only its classical instances (defect zero, normal defect group); the papers' proofs (reduction theorems, the classification) are beyond it.
