# 33. Domain of dependence / development for compressible Euler

## Status
UNPLANNED. Quasilinear hyperbolic systems, compressible Euler, developments and their uniqueness (Proposition 33.4) appear in no merged roadmap or open PR. Open PR #2232 (LorentzianGeometry) supplies the Lorentzian side: Lorentzian metrics with locally Lipschitz causal curves for continuous metrics (#50), null hypersurfaces (#51), and domains of dependence `D^±(A)` and Cauchy horizons `H^±(A)` for smooth spacetimes (§C6.2); it does not define 33.2's domain of dependence relative to a region with boundary for a `C¹` acoustical metric, nor 33.3–33.5.

## Mathlib
- Has: ODE/Lipschitz curve basics, `LipschitzWith`, a.e. derivatives of Lipschitz functions (Rademacher), `AddCircle`.
- Missing: Lorentzian metrics, causal/null vectors and curves, domains of dependence, Euler equations, symmetric hyperbolic systems.

## Tau Ceti code
- Nothing for causal structure or compressible fluids (grep for causal curve, domain of dependence, compressible: no hits). `Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/AlongCurve/NondegenerateMetric.lean` handles nondegenerate (pseudo-Riemannian) metrics along curves only.
- Incompressible Euler work is only in draft PR 237 (below).

## Merged roadmaps
- None. LocalVariationalFieldTheory LVF3.CauchyPhase has classical Cauchy data for Lagrangian field theories (Klein–Gordon), not causal domains of dependence. No "symmetric hyperbolic" or "Lorentzian causal" targets.

## Open roadmap PRs
- PR 1772 "Relativistic quantum field theory and perturbative renormalization" §1.2: flat (Minkowski) hyperbolic Cauchy theory and finite propagation speed for Klein–Gordon. Constant-coefficient, flat metric only; not a target for 33.
- PR 237 "Add incompressible flows and Navier–Stokes roadmap" (draft): incompressible only.
- PR #2232 "Add semi-Riemannian foundations and Lorentzian causality roadmap": the acoustical metric as a Lorentzian metric and its time orientation by `B = ∂_t + v^i ∂_i` (§F1.2 and §C2.1 Checks), future causal curves, locally Lipschitz causal curves for continuous metrics and past-inextendibility (§C2.2; 33.1's curve class), null hypersurfaces and the acoustical eikonal equation (§C3.2), achronal sets (§C6.1), and `D^±(A)`, `D(A)`, Cauchy horizons `H^±(A)` with their null generators and Cauchy surfaces (§C6.2, Conventions) for smooth time-oriented spacetimes without boundary. Not a target for 33: 33.2 is relative to a region `D ⊆ {t ≥ t₀}` with boundary and a `C¹` metric, and developments of the Euler data (33.3–33.5) and their uniqueness need hyperbolic PDE, which PR #2232 lists as outside it.

## Prerequisite chain
1. Lipschitz curves, a.e. differentiability: Mathlib.
2. Lorentzian (time-oriented) metric on ℝ×Σ, causal/null vectors, causal curves: PR #2232 §§C0.1–C0.2, §F1.2, §§C2.1–C2.2 (#50).
3. Future domain of dependence D⁺_D(S): PR #2232 §C6.2 has `D^+(A)` for smooth spacetimes without boundary; the relative version in a region with boundary for `C¹` metrics: unplanned.
4. Null hypersurfaces (Definition #51): PR #2232 §C3.2.
5. Compressible Euler system, acoustical metric, developments, Prop 33.4 uniqueness (energy estimates for quasilinear symmetric hyperbolic systems in lens-shaped domains): unplanned.
6. Local well-posedness in H^s, s > n/2 + 1 (for existence of developments): unplanned (PDE roadmap has no hyperbolic layer).

## Library-scale gap
Lorentzian causal theory (causal curves, J^±, domains of dependence, Cauchy horizons; Hawking–Ellis Ch. 6, O'Neill *Semi-Riemannian Geometry* Ch. 14) is PR #2232, for smooth spacetimes in its global sections. A "HyperbolicSystems" roadmap would need, on top of it, domains of dependence in regions with boundary for low-regularity metrics, and the energy method for first-order symmetric hyperbolic systems (Friedrichs; local existence, uniqueness and finite speed of propagation in H^s; Majda *Compressible Fluid Flow and Systems of Conservation Laws*, Taylor *PDE III* Ch. 16), with compressible Euler and its acoustical metric as the model instance (Christodoulou, *The Formation of Shocks in 3-Dimensional Fluids*). It would sit beside PDE (consuming Sobolev/Bochner layers) and next to LocalVariationalFieldTheory.
