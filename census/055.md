PARTIAL-CODE. Merged [GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters/README.md) specifies the definition in full: weighted variation and perimeter `P_μ` for any positive `C¹` density `e^{−W}`, with `gaussianPerimeter` the Gaussian case (§2.1, 55.1–55.2) and its identification with `e^{−W}μHE[n−1]` on the reduced boundary (§2.2); `q`-clusters with total perimeter `½ Σ P_μ(Ω_i)` (§3.1, 55.3), interfaces `Σ_ij = ∂*Ω_i ∩ ∂*Ω_j` and `P_μ(Ω) = Σ_{i<j} A_ij` (§3.2, 55.4), isoperimetric profiles (§3.4) and simplicial clusters (§§4.1–4.2, 55.5). Definition 55.1 is in Tau Ceti code (`TauCeti.gaussianPerimeter`, `Analysis/BoundedVariation/Weighted/Basic.lean`) with the elementary laws of Proposition 55.2 (null-set invariance, complement), its density-bounded form the identity `V_μ(u; Ω) = ∫_Ω e^{−W} d|Du|` (`weightedVariation_eq_lintegral_variationMeasure`) and, in dimension `n ≥ 2`, the reduced-boundary form `P_μ(U) = ∫_{∂*U} e^{−W} dHⁿ⁻¹` of 55.2 (`weightedPerimeter_eq_lintegral_reducedBoundary`, `Weighted/ReducedBoundary.lean`), and so are the `q`-clusters of 55.3 with their total perimeter (`TauCeti.Cluster W q`, `Cluster.perimeter`, `Weighted/Cluster/Basic.lean`) and, for a `C¹` weight in dimension `n ≥ 2`, the interfaces and interface areas of 55.4 with `P_μ(Ω) = Σ_{i<j} A_ij` (`Cluster.interface`, `Cluster.interfaceArea`, `Cluster.perimeter_eq_sum_interfaceArea`, `Weighted/Cluster/Interface/Basic.lean`), as is the lower semicontinuity of 55.2 (`gaussianPerimeter_le_liminf`, `Weighted/LowerSemicontinuity.lean`); the Lipschitz-domain clause of 55.2 and the simplicial clusters of 55.5 are not (the isoperimetric profile of 55.5 and the existence of minimizing clusters are, `TauCeti.isoperimetricProfile`, `exists_cluster_perimeter_eq_isoperimetricProfile`), so the definition is partly in code (a borderline call: the carrier, 55.1–55.4 and the profile are in code, the simplicial clusters of 55.5 are not). De Giorgi's structure theorem, the GeometricMeasureTheory G5 input of §§2.2 and 3.2, is in code. The roadmap proves the two-cell case of the Milman–Neeman theorem and leaves `3 ≤ q ≤ n+1` to a Part II.
