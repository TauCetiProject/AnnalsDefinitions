## Tau Ceti code
- `TauCeti.StrictConvexBilliardTable o` (`TauCeti/Dynamics/Billiard/NextCollision.lean`): an `L`-periodic unit-speed map `ℝ → E` into an oriented Euclidean plane, injective on `[0, L)`, with `0 < ω(χ'(s), χ(t) − χ(s))` whenever `χ(t) ≠ χ(s)` (strict convexity with positive orientation). Only a unit-speed derivative is assumed, so `C²` tables of positive curvature (Setup 92.1) are instances. It is the condition of MathematicalBilliards §1.1's `ConvexTable` (stated there in `ℂ`), which the roadmap does not cite.
- Same file: `outgoingDir` (the unit velocity `v χ'(s) + √(1 − v²) J χ'(s)`), `exists_collision`, `nextParam` with `eq_nextParam_of_areaForm_eq_zero` (the unique exit parameter in `(s, s + L)`, Proposition 92.2's ray statement), `nextTangential_mem_Ioo` (the next chord is not tangent), `outgoingDir_nextCollision` (reflection law), `nextCollision` and `nextCollision_add_period` (equivariance under the deck translation). The tangential coordinate `v = cos δ` is the convention of the first Remark.

3. Closed cylinder, twist maps of the annulus, non-wandering set: MathematicalBilliards §2.3 and Layer 4 (merged, not implemented; open Tau Ceti PRs for §4.1 and §4.4), with the rest of Layers 1–3 on the carrier `ConvexTable` (open Tau Ceti PRs for §1.1).

5. Central symmetry, caustics, Birkhoff–Poritsky: unplanned (named as a downstream consumer of MathematicalBilliards Layers 1–5).

## Library-scale gap
The twist-map theory around the billiard map (Birkhoff's graph theorem, rotation numbers of invariant curves, Birkhoff periodic orbits) is the merged MathematicalBilliards (Tabachnikov, *Geometry and Billiards*; Katok–Hasselblatt ch. 9 and 13; Herman 1983). What remains for the definition is implementation of its Layers 1–5; the Tau Ceti carrier `StrictConvexBilliardTable` and its next-collision map, inverse, homeomorphism, generating function and invariant measure cover most of Layers 1–3 in the `(s, cos δ)` coordinates and an oriented Euclidean plane rather than `ℂ`. The paper's theorem further needs caustics, central symmetry and the Birkhoff–Poritsky argument, which MathematicalBilliards names as a downstream roadmap (Bialy–Mironov; Siburg, *The Principle of Least Action in Geometry and Dynamics*).
