Sheffield's uniqueness of the ergodic Gibbs measure of a given interior slope, which makes 100.5's "the" measure well defined, is left to the named DimerModelsAndLozengeTilingsPartII; the definition is stateable through §9.7's explicit measure, which is the paper's object. Tau Ceti has only the lattice and its dual hexagonal graph (§§1.1–1.2, `TauCeti.TriEdge`, `TauCeti.hexGraph`) and the incomplete beta kernel (§9.6, `incompleteBetaKernel`); tilings, specifications, Gibbs measures and slopes are not in code.

Implemented: §§1.1–1.2 and the kernel of §9.6 (above); the rest is not.
  - Layer 1 (README l.222): §1.1 vertices, edges (`TriEdge`), faces and symmetries of the triangular lattice; §1.2 the dual hexagonal graph `hexGraph`; §§1.3–1.4 regions and tilings; §1.5 tilings are perfect matchings (100.1).
  - Layer 2: edge cochains and combinatorial simple connectivity, heights of plane tilings (§2.5, Prop 100.2), heights on regions, boundary heights, flips, Thurston's criterion.
  - Layer 3: Kasteleyn matrices, Kasteleyn's theorem, local statistics on finite simply connected regions.
  - Layer 4 (l.836): outside and tail σ-algebras, the weak topology on a finite-alphabet subshift, §4.5 `Specification X` (proper consistent Markov kernels) and `Specification.IsGibbs`, §4.6 the face property and closedness, for configuration spaces with a proper action of a countable group.
  - Layer 5: the dimer specification on locally finite periodic graphs (§§5.1–5.3, `dimerGibbsInvariant_isExtreme`); §5.4 the lozenge instance `IsLozengeGibbs`, `lozengeGibbsInvariant`, `lozengeGibbs_extreme_iff_ergodic` (100.3.1–100.3.2).
  - Layer 6: §6.1 `slope μ` (100.3.3), §§6.2–6.4 corners, edges of the slope triangle, extreme points of slope fibres.
  - Layers 7–9: torus tilings and torus limits, the four-determinant Kasteleyn formula, the infinite inverse Kasteleyn kernel, §9.5 `exists_ergodicGibbs_of_slope` (existence in 100.4), §§9.6–9.7 the complex slope `ξ`, the incomplete beta kernel and `incompleteBetaProcess_exists_unique`.
  - Layer 10: local convergence, limits of uniform measures are Gibbs (§10.2), §10.3 the lattice side of Aggarwal's universality theorem.
  - Downstream consumers: DimerModelsAndLozengeTilingsPartII owns Sheffield's uniqueness of the ergodic Gibbs measure of each interior slope, the variational principle, limit shapes and Aggarwal's theorem itself. Lattice spin systems with finite single-site spaces consume Layer 4.
  - No roadmap suppliers ("From other roadmaps: None").
- TensorProductCombinatorics (README ~l.1590) has finite puzzles as tilings by triangles/rhombi (Knutson-Tao puzzles), combinatorial only. OperatorAlgebraDynamics D9 has quantum translation-invariant states and the variational principle, not DLR measures on tilings. TemperleyLiebOperatorAlgebras (merged PR #867) has finite Potts–Fortuin–Kasteleyn partition functions, not the Kasteleyn dimer method. ## Prerequisite chain
1. Triangular lattice graph on ℤ², faces, lozenge tilings as perfect matchings of the hexagonal dual (100.1): Mathlib graph theory; DimerModelsAndLozengeTilings Layer 1 (§§1.1–1.2 in Tau Ceti: `TriEdge`, `TriFace`, `hexGraph`).
2. Tiling space as closed subset of `{0,1}^{E}`, compact metrizable, shift action of ℤ²: Mathlib (product topology); DimerModelsAndLozengeTilings §4.4, §5.1.
3. Height function `H_M` (100.2): DimerModelsAndLozengeTilings §2.5.
4. Gibbs (uniform DLR) property (100.3.1): DimerModelsAndLozengeTilings §4.5 (specifications as Mathlib kernels), §5.4 (`IsLozengeGibbs` in cylinder form).
5. Translation invariance and ergodicity under ℤ² (100.3.2): Mathlib `ErgodicVAdd`/`ErgodicSMul`; extreme-point equivalence for countable-group actions in Tau Ceti (`ErgodicSMul.iff_mem_extremePoints`).
6. Slope (100.3.3): DimerModelsAndLozengeTilings §6.1. Existence in Theorem 100.4 and the determinantal kernel (Kenyon–Okounkov–Sheffield, Okounkov–Reshetikhin): DimerModelsAndLozengeTilings §§9.5–9.7. Sheffield's uniqueness (100.4, needed for 100.5's "the"): unplanned (the roadmap's named Part II).

## Library-scale gap
The merged DimerModelsAndLozengeTilings is the roadmap for this definition (Kenyon, "Lectures on dimers"; Gorin, *Lectures on Random Lozenge Tilings*; Georgii, *Gibbs Measures and Phase Transitions*; Kenyon–Okounkov–Sheffield; Okounkov–Reshetikhin): what remains is its implementation, §§1.3–1.5 and Layers 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 in turn (§§1.1–1.2 and the §9.6 kernel are in code). Sheffield's uniqueness theorem (*Random Surfaces*), the variational principle, limit shapes and the universality theorem of the cited paper are its named Part II.
