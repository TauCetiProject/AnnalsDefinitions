# 8. Glauberman correspondence

## Status
ROADMAP-MERGED. Merged [ModularCharactersAndBlocksPartII](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCharactersAndBlocksPartII/README.md) (PR #2411) specifies the whole definition: the action of `S` on `Irr(Γ)` and `Irr_S(Γ)` (§1.1), fixed classes under a coprime action and Brauer's permutation lemma (§§1.2–1.3), Gallagher's normalised extension and the canonical extension to `Γ ⋊ S` (§§2.1–2.2), the cyclic case (Layer 3), the `p`-group case with its unique constituent of multiplicity prime to `p` (§§4.2, 4.5, Theorem 8.1), and, for solvable `S`, existence, independence of the series and uniqueness of the compatible family of bijections `π(Γ, S) : Irr_S(Γ) → Irr(C_Γ(S))` defining `θ ↦ θ*` (§§4.3–4.5, Theorem 8.2 and Definition 8.3), with equivariance (§4.6) and Glauberman degree-divisibility as a proposition (§5.2). Of it, Brauer's permutation lemma (§1.3), Gallagher's normalised extension (§2.1), Glauberman's canonical extension (§2.2), Glauberman's Lemma 1 and the values of the canonical extension (§2.3), Gallagher's correspondence (§2.4) and Schur's theorem for projective representations and the degree ratio over a normal subgroup (§2.5(b)–(c)) are in code, as is `Irr_S(Γ)` with its characterising API (§1.1); the fixed-class lemmas and the correspondence itself are not. Its other inputs in code: complex character theory with restriction, inner products and Clifford theory (Tau Ceti), Schur–Zassenhaus (Mathlib).

## Mathlib
- Has: `MulDistribMulAction` (actions by automorphisms), `FixedPoints.subgroup`, `Subgroup.exists_right_complement'_of_coprime` (Schur–Zassenhaus, `GroupTheory/SchurZassenhaus.lean`), `IsSolvable`, `SemidirectProduct`, `FDRep.character` with orthonormality.
- Missing: action of `S` on `Irr(Γ)`, coprime-action lemmas beyond Schur–Zassenhaus, the correspondence.

## Library-scale gap
None: merged ModularCharactersAndBlocksPartII (Isaacs, *Character Theory of Finite Groups*, Ch. 13; Navarro, *Character Theory and the McKay Conjecture*, Ch. 2 and 5; Glauberman 1968) is the roadmap, and what remains for the definition is implementation. The paper itself (Geck) also needs Deligne–Lusztig Green functions of finite reductive groups: merged [FiniteReductiveGroups](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/RiemannHypothesisForDirichletLFunctions/FiniteReductiveGroups/README.md) §4.2 defines them for every connected reductive group over a finite field, with the character formula (§4.3) and their values at regular unipotent elements (§4.10); Green polynomials and character sheaves are outside that roadmap (see #2).
